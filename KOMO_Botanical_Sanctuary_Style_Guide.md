# KOMO Botanical Sanctuary
## Comprehensive brand, art direction and digital style guide

**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 5 November 2026  
**Status:** Proposed working specification for review and implementation  
**Core direction:** Warm, tactile and restorative, with enough olive contrast to feel distinctive.

This guide translates the direction developed in our conversation into a reusable system for designers, photographers, copywriters, developers and content teams. It is grounded in the supplied photograph of KOMO's outlet and its existing Health, Joy, Beauty and Vitality philosophy. Values, layouts and ratios below are proposed specifications, not measurements extracted from the existing website or evidence of customer performance.

The outlet photograph takes precedence over the earlier illustrative moodboard where they differ. Use its light cream surfaces, pale timber, broad curves, warm illumination and contemporary character as the physical reference. Generated campaign images are concepts, not documentation of real customers, staff, facilities, packaging or treatment outcomes.

## 1. Brand foundation

### 1.1 Positioning

KOMO offers personalised scalp and hair care within a restorative spa experience, combining attentive consultation, appropriate care and time to unwind.

### 1.2 Emotional promise

**A place to unwind and feel cared for.**

The experience should help a customer feel listened to, comfortable with their choices and welcome to return. The visual identity communicates these qualities through real human interaction and a recognisable sense of place.

### 1.3 Message hierarchy

| Level | Message | Supporting evidence to show |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Personalised scalp care. A restorative ritual. | Consultation followed by considered hands-on care |
| Emotional | Time to feel cared for | Attentive staff, comfortable treatment and hospitality |
| Practical | Understand the service before booking | Clear options, durations, inclusions and prices |
| Credibility | Care explained clearly | Authentic techniques, relevant formulation information and substantiated claims |
| Action | Explore treatments or request an appointment | Straightforward treatment selection and booking |

### 1.4 Personality

- **Attentive:** listens, explains and notices details.
- **Calm:** gives information without urgency or pressure.
- **Knowledgeable:** answers concrete questions in accessible language.
- **Contemporary:** clean, light and welcoming.
- **Considered:** quality is evident in consistency and execution.

### 1.5 Brand boundaries

KOMO's scalp wellness services must be described according to their actual scope. Do not imply medical diagnosis, medical treatment, guaranteed regrowth or permanent results through copy, imagery or interface labels. Describe AI-assisted assessment according to its verified capabilities. Do not present botanical origin as proof of safety or effectiveness.

## 2. Creative principles

| Principle | Practical rule |
|---|---|
| Warmth | Use welcoming light and human interaction while preserving neutral skin tones |
| Tactility | Show linen, water, hair and material texture at an appropriate scale |
| Rest | Give photographs and text room to breathe |
| Contrast | Use deep olive to anchor light compositions and maintain legibility |
| Personal care | Include listening and explanation alongside relaxing treatment moments |
| Authenticity | Show the actual location, equipment and process wherever possible |
| Recognition | Repeat the logo, palette, Flow shape and composition rules consistently |

Every main composition should communicate attentive care, restorative comfort or both. Decorative foliage alone is insufficient to tell the KOMO story.

## 3. Audience and customer journey

These are proposed creative audiences to validate against customer and booking data.

| Audience motivation | Question | Content response |
|---|---|---|
| A restorative break | Will I feel comfortable here? | Atmospheric treatment footage and visit guidance |
| A scalp concern | Will someone understand my needs? | Consultation, clear service scope and treatment explanations |
| First-time curiosity | What happens, and what does it cost? | A complete first-visit journey and transparent menu |
| Ongoing care | Why should I return? | Consistent service, appropriate care guidance and familiar people |

### Journey objectives

1. **Discovery:** recognise KOMO and understand its appeal.
2. **Consideration:** understand what the service involves and whether it fits.
3. **Booking:** see price, duration, terms and the next step.
4. **Arrival:** encounter the same warmth shown online.
5. **Care:** feel informed, comfortable and attended to.
6. **Return:** remember a worthwhile experience and know how to book again.

Art direction supports recognition and desire. Repeat visits depend on the actual service and value delivered.

## 4. Health, Joy, Beauty and Vitality

Retain the four existing pillars as emotional themes. Give each a distinct photographic purpose.

| Pillar | Meaning | Primary image | Supporting subject | Suggested short description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Health | Understanding and caring for the scalp | Therapist discussing a scalp assessment with a customer | Care explanation and appropriate home routines | Care that begins with understanding your scalp. |
| Joy | Enjoying time that feels your own | Customer relaxed during a gentle massage | Welcome, comfort and hospitality | A little time to settle, unwind and enjoy. |
| Beauty | Personal comfort and confidence | Natural mirror moment after care | Believable hair texture and individual expression | Care for your hair. Confidence in yourself. |
| Vitality | A refreshed feeling associated with the visit | Customer leaving with relaxed posture and natural movement | Return to everyday life | A restorative pause before returning to your day. |

### Four-pillar image set

- Use one customer across a single campaign to tell a connected story.
- Include different customers, ages, genders and hair textures across the broader library.
- Alternate consultation, treatment detail, reflection and wider departure views.
- Use consistent grading, location details and image proportions.
- Put labels below photographs rather than across faces.
- Keep pillar meanings visible even when labels are removed.
- Treat Vitality as an experience theme, not a physiological outcome claim.

## 6. Logo and supporting identity

### 6.1 Master artwork

Use the supplied official KOMO master logo. Do not reconstruct it from a typeface, trace a photograph or treat lettering in a generated moodboard as approved artwork. A redesign is outside this guide's scope.

### 6.2 Proposed handling rules

- Use the approved olive or original-colour version on light backgrounds.
- Use an approved reversed version on dark olive or dark photography.
- Preserve aspect ratio and original letter relationships.
- Start with clear space equal to half the logo's capital height on all sides; replace this with official rules if provided.
- Begin digital tests at 100px wide and print tests at 25mm wide. Increase the size whenever small descriptor text becomes unclear.
- Use a descriptor-free master variant at small sizes only if that variant is approved.
- Never add glow, shadows, outlines or gradients to compensate for a poor background.

### 6.3 Placement

Website navigation: upper left. Social campaign tiles: upper right within a consistent safe margin. Packaging and signage: place according to the surface and viewing distance. Do not force one placement rule onto every medium.

KOMO Flow is a supporting visual device. It must not replace the logo in identification-critical applications until recognition has been tested.

## 7. KOMO Flow

### 7.1 Origin and meaning

Develop a master curve from the outlet's flowing ceiling light. The associations are continuity, water, hair and gentle movement. The current concept is an exploration, not final vector artwork.

### 7.2 Construction brief

- Draw one open, asymmetric curve with broad, unhurried bends.
- Keep the silhouette simple enough to recognise when cropped.
- Avoid closed leaf shapes, droplets, literal hair strands and unrelated swirls.
- Preserve a fixed aspect ratio in each approved variant.
- Produce a primary ribbon form and a simplified small-scale form.
- Test the shape as a silhouette before adding texture or motion.

### 7.3 Permitted applications

| Application | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Website | One broad transition or cropped decorative anchor |
| Social cover | A controlled curve alongside the headline or image edge |
| Print | Debossed detail or simple ink treatment |
| Packaging | A consistent band or supporting surface detail |
| Motion | A gentle reveal or movement following its own path |

Use one prominent Flow gesture per composition as the default. Cropping is allowed; stretching, flipping and changing proportions require a defined variant. Do not cross faces, treatment actions, labels or body copy. Do not use thin hairlines that disappear in small exports or printing.

## 8. Colour system

### 8.1 Core palette

| Token | Name | Hex | Intended role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `brand-olive` | Forest olive | `#34463A` | Main text, primary actions and contrast sections |
| `canvas` | Warm ivory | `#F4F0E7` | Main backgrounds and printed canvas |
| `brand-sage` | Muted sage | `#A6B29A` | Supporting surfaces and decorative Flow |
| `brand-stone` | Warm stone | `#C9BCAE` | Secondary material accents |
| `surface` | Soft white | `#FFFDFA` | Forms, cards and layered surfaces |
| `text-secondary` | Muted olive | `#526055` | Secondary text on light backgrounds |
| `border` | Soft neutral | `#D8D8CC` | Dividers and inactive boundaries |
| `action-hover` | Deep olive | `#28392E` | Hover and active action states |

### 8.2 Distribution

For designed layouts excluding photography, start with 60% ivory/soft white, 20% olive, 12% sage and 8% stone. These are composition guides, not rigid quotas. The lighter distribution supersedes the earlier darker moodboard balance.

### 8.3 Functional colours

| Role | Starting token | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Error | `#9C3535` | Error text plus a clear written explanation |
| Success | `#315C40` | Confirmation text and status |
| Warning | `#775112` | Cautions with explicit wording |
| Focus | `#34463A` | Visible keyboard outline on light surfaces |

These are functional accents, not campaign colours. Verify their contrast in the actual component combinations before release.

### 8.4 Colour pairing rules

- Prefer olive on ivory and ivory on olive for primary copy.
- Use olive text over sage or stone only after checking the implemented contrast.
- Do not use sage or stone as small text on ivory.
- Use a controlled scrim or dedicated text panel when text sits on a photograph.
- Check overlays on every responsive crop and video frame that carries text.
- Never communicate availability, selection or errors through colour alone.

### 8.5 Print

The hex values are digital references. Obtain printer-specific colour conversions and proofs for the chosen paper and process. Do not assume a universal CMYK equivalent. Proof olive for loss of detail and ivory for an unwanted yellow cast.

## 9. Typography

### 9.1 Hierarchy and font policy

Use a warm, readable sans serif for information and interfaces, supported by an editorial serif for short emotional headlines. This balances the contemporary outlet with the restorative creative direction.

**Implementable baseline:** Georgia for editorial headlines; a system sans stack for body copy and UI. These provide a working hierarchy without requiring an unverified external font licence. They are not a claim that the generated concept uses those exact fonts.

```css
--font-editorial: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif;
--font-body: system-ui, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, 'Segoe UI', sans-serif;
```

For a final branded font selection, test candidate families in the actual logo context, website and campaign templates, confirm licensing, and replace the tokens centrally. Until selected, do not mix multiple experimental fonts across published work.

### 9.2 Type scale

| Role | Desktop | Mobile | Weight | Line height |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign display | 64px | 40px | Regular serif | 1.08–1.15 |
| Page heading | 52px | 36px | Regular serif | 1.15 |
| Section heading | 40px | 30px | Regular serif | 1.2 |
| Card heading | 24px | 22px | Medium sans | 1.3 |
| Subheading | 20px | 18px | Medium sans | 1.4 |
| Body | 18px | 16px | Regular sans | 1.6 |
| Supporting text | 16px | 14px | Regular sans | 1.5 |
| Button/navigation | 16px | 16px | Medium sans | 1.4 |
| Eyebrow | 12px | 12px | Medium sans | 1.4 |

Use fluid sizing between endpoints. Keep body measures around 45–75 characters. Reserve uppercase tracking of approximately `0.08em` for short eyebrow labels. Use sentence case elsewhere. Avoid very thin weights, justified paragraphs, long italic text and serif body paragraphs in interfaces.

### 9.3 Content length

Hero headline: ideally 4–9 words. Hero supporting copy: 1–2 short sentences. Card descriptions: roughly 20–40 words. Treatment explanations can be longer where needed for a clear decision. Do not cut essential qualifications merely to achieve a tidy card.

## 10. Layout and spacing

### 10.1 Grid

| Context | Specification |
|---|---|
| Desktop, 1024px and above | 12-column grid; maximum content width 1200px |
| Tablet, 768–1023px | 8-column grid; 32px outer gutters |
| Mobile, below 768px | 4-column grid; 20–24px outer gutters |
| Main grid gap | 24–32px desktop; 16–24px mobile |
| Reading column | Maximum approximately 720px |

Breakpoints are initial implementation values. Adjust where content genuinely stops fitting, rather than targeting a particular device name.

### 10.2 Spacing scale

Use `4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96px`.

- Within a field or label group: 8–12px.
- Between related elements: 16–24px.
- Card padding: 24–32px desktop; 20–24px mobile.
- Section padding: 80–96px desktop; 48–64px mobile.
- Major story transition: up to 120px when supported by the composition.

### 10.3 Shape and depth

Buttons: 8px radius. Inputs: 8px. Standard cards: 12px. Small tags: 4px. Use architectural arches sparingly for campaign images, with a reusable mask. Never crop a face or important treatment action to satisfy the mask.

Prefer colour and spacing to heavy shadows. If depth is needed, use a subtle shadow such as `0 8px 24px rgba(52,70,58,0.06)`. Keep floating controls visually clear without making every card appear elevated.

## 11. Photography

### 11.1 Image language

Warm, natural, attentive and specific to KOMO. Capture care in progress and the place in which it happens. Include recognisable details such as curved walls, the ceiling light, reception and mirrors.

### 11.2 Subjects

| Subject | Desired emphasis |
|---|---|
| Consultation | Listening, explanation and collaboration |
| Treatment | Careful hands, supported posture and authentic technique |
| Sensory detail | Water, lather, linen and natural hair texture |
| People | Relaxed expressions and believable interaction |
| Interior | Light, curves, circulation and a welcoming atmosphere |
| Products | Real packaging, clear labels and material quality |
| Departure | Ease and natural movement |

### 11.3 Lighting and grading

- Use soft directional light with controlled highlights.
- Preserve the warm feeling of the outlet while correcting excessive orange/yellow casts.
- Keep skin tones natural across different complexions.
- Keep olive consistent and prevent greens reflecting strongly onto skin.
- Preserve detail in dark hair, pale towels and illuminated signage.
- Avoid heavy haze, crushed shadows, glossy skin smoothing and exaggerated hair shine.
- Grade the complete campaign together; one preset will not fix every lighting condition.

### 11.4 Composition

Capture a wide, medium and close view for each important scene. Leave intentional negative space in hero candidates. Photograph both left- and right-weighted compositions. Keep text away from faces, hands and key equipment. Provide safe crops for 16:9, 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16.

## 13. Image selection and production

### 13.1 Selection criteria

An image should answer at least one question: What is the service? What happens here? Who cares for me? How might the experience feel? What makes this recognisably KOMO?

### 13.2 AI imagery

AI imagery may support early concept development, mood exploration and clearly illustrative campaign concepts. Inspect hands, faces, hair, reflections, equipment and text. Never use generated people as customer testimonials or generated scenes as proof of the actual outlet or service outcomes. Do not invent qualifications, product labels or treatment mechanisms.

### 13.3 Retouching

Remove temporary distractions where appropriate. Preserve identity, age characteristics, skin texture and believable hair density. Do not create an implied treatment outcome by adding hair, changing density or altering scalp appearance.

### 13.4 Exports

| Asset | Starting export |
|---|---|
| Web hero | AVIF/WebP with fallback as needed; responsive widths around 960, 1440 and 1920px |
| Web card | Responsive widths around 480, 800 and 1200px |
| Feed portrait | 1080 × 1350px working template |
| Square | 1080 × 1080px working template |
| Vertical video/Story | 1080 × 1920px working template |
| Print | Dimensions and resolution agreed with printer; typically 300ppi at final size |

Platform crops and interface overlays can change. Preview current placement before publishing. Keep editable originals and never repeatedly compress exported files. For websites, encode dimensions, supply responsive sources, lazy-load below-fold images and prioritise the main hero image appropriately.

## 14. Graphics and illustration

Use graphics when they improve understanding: treatment sequence, product use or a service comparison. Keep diagrams simple, accurate and labelled. Use olive strokes on ivory with consistent weight. Avoid pseudo-scientific decoration, molecule imagery without meaning, ornamental leaves on every card and unverified scalp scores.

Do not place emojis or decorative icons on premium campaign tiles by default. Where UI icons support navigation, pair unfamiliar ones with text and use one consistent family.

## 15. Website structure and storytelling

This is a recommended content sequence to adapt to the existing site. It does not authorise deleting approved pages or rebuilding the platform.

### 15.1 Homepage sequence

1. **Opening:** emotional headline, practical service description, location and primary action.
2. **The experience:** explain consultation, personalised care and restorative time.
3. **Treatment options:** distinguish scalp care, scalp spa and hair care using verified service categories.
4. **Personalisation:** show how customer needs inform the appointment.
5. **Four pillars:** Health, Joy, Beauty and Vitality with distinct imagery.
6. **The outlet:** show the actual setting and visit information.
7. **First-visit guidance:** preparation, duration, prices and expectations.
8. **Booking invitation:** clear next step and contact options.

### 15.2 Hero example

**Headline:** Let the day fall away.  
**Supporting copy:** Personalised scalp and hair care in a calm, welcoming space at Wheelock Place.  
**Primary action:** Explore treatments  
**Secondary action:** Request an appointment

Confirm current location details and service wording before publication. Text should remain usable when the video does not load.

### 15.3 Treatment page specification

Each service page should show its name, plain-language purpose, intended customer needs, duration, actual inclusions, price and applicable terms, step-by-step process, realistic expectations, relevant suitability information, location and booking action. Explain how the appointment is confirmed. Separate introductory offers from the standard menu.

### 15.4 Credibility content

Connect each claim to relevant evidence. Explain what a certification covers and which entity or product holds it. Patents, manufacturing systems and awards should not substitute for explaining what the customer receives.

## 16. Interface components

| Component | Visual and interaction specification |
|---|---|
| Header | Clear logo, restrained navigation, one main appointment action; maintain contrast over media |
| Primary button | Olive fill, ivory text, 8px radius, minimum 48px height; concise action label |
| Secondary button | Transparent/light surface, olive border and text; visible hover and focus |
| Text link | Olive with underline or another persistent non-colour cue |
| Treatment card | Real image, distinct name, purpose, verified duration/price and clear next action |
| Pillar card | Image, heading, short explanation; no misleading clickable appearance |
| Accordion | Semantic button, clear expanded state, keyboard operable; concise question |
| Form field | Persistent label, 8px radius, generous padding, visible focus and error explanation |
| Selection | Text plus clear selected state; never rely on green alone |
| Status message | Plain-language status; announced accessibly where appropriate |
| Floating contact | Small, labelled and clear of content, mobile navigation and consent controls |

### 16.1 States

Every interactive component needs default, hover where applicable, keyboard focus, active, disabled where relevant, loading and error states. Loading must not create duplicate requests. Retain entered form values after recoverable errors.

### 16.2 Appointment form

Ask only for useful information: name, contact method, contact details, treatment interest and preferred appointment timing where supported. Make optional fields clear. Avoid asking for detailed sensitive information in an unrestricted message box. Explain that a request requires confirmation if the system does not reserve a slot. The success message must match actual system behaviour.

### 16.3 Keyboard focus

Use a visible 2–3px outline with separation from the control. On dark surfaces, use an appropriate contrasting outline. Do not remove the browser focus indicator without an accessible replacement.

## 17. Motion and sound

| Context | Starting direction |
|---|---|
| Button response | 150–200ms colour transition |
| Content reveal | 250–400ms subtle opacity/position change |
| Flow animation | 2–4 seconds for a restrained reveal |
| Website video | Quiet, stable framing and a still-image fallback |
| Social sensory edit | Let water, touch and treatment sounds establish atmosphere |

Avoid scroll hijacking, large parallax effects, repeated floating elements, flashing light and compulsory sound. Do not delay content access for an animation. Respect reduced-motion preferences, provide pause controls for relevant moving content and caption spoken video. Use music only with appropriate rights.

## 18. Voice and copy

### 18.1 Voice rules

Write in British English. Use short, connected sentences, concrete details and restrained confidence. Explain rather than impress. Avoid em dashes, excessive exclamation marks, pressure tactics and vague strings of luxury adjectives.

| Quality | Application |
|---|---|
| Warm | Acknowledge the person and their questions |
| Clear | Explain what the treatment includes |
| Knowledgeable | Use accurate terms and explain unfamiliar ones |
| Respectful | Avoid embarrassment, fear and appearance-based judgement |
| Transparent | State price, terms and confirmation steps clearly |

### 18.2 Copy examples

| Avoid | Preferred approach |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed hair transformation | Describe the service and realistic individual expectations |
| The ultimate revolutionary scalp solution | Explain the actual care process |
| Your scalp is ageing badly | Ask what the customer has noticed without judgement |
| Natural means completely safe | Describe ingredients accurately and relevant suitability considerations |
| Book now before it is too late | Explore treatments / Request an appointment |
| Standardised SOP excellence | Explain the consistent steps customers can expect |

### 18.3 Vocabulary

Prefer: personalised care, scalp assessment, attentive care, restorative pause, scalp spa, comfortable, refreshed, treatment options. Use these according to context, not as repetitive filler.

Verify before using: diagnosis, clinically proven, hair regrowth, follicle renewal, anti-ageing, medical, guaranteed, safest, best and permanent. A disclaimer cannot repair a misleading headline.

### 18.4 Offer copy

Offers must identify the service, price, eligibility, inclusions, exclusions, expiry if applicable and relevant terms. Show taxes and charges clearly according to applicable requirements. Keep offers visually subordinate to the brand experience across the content mix.

## 22. Accessibility and inclusive presentation

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the proposed website acceptance target, with a proper implementation audit rather than a claim of compliance from this document alone.

- Check at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text.
- Check applicable 3:1 requirements for interface boundaries and meaningful graphics.
- Keep keyboard operation, visible focus and logical reading order.
- Use semantic headings and clear control names.
- Design touch controls around a generous 44–48px target where practical.
- Support text resizing, reflow and zoom without losing essential actions.
- Write alt text describing the relevant image content. Decorative Flow artwork should have empty alt text or be hidden appropriately.
- Use captions for speech and meaningful audio information.
- Avoid mandatory motion and automatically playing sound.
- Include different genders, ages, skin tones and hair textures without stereotyping particular concerns.

**Example alt text:** Therapist discussing a scalp assessment with a seated customer.

## 24. Production workflow and asset management

1. Confirm service facts, intended audience and the required customer action.
2. Choose the relevant pillar and content template.
3. Select authentic assets or commission the required photography.
4. Apply approved logo artwork, tokens and Flow variants.
5. Review copy, factual claims and treatment representation.
6. Test full-size and small-screen presentation, crops and contrast.
7. Export using channel-specific settings and retain editable masters.
8. Publish through the authorised workflow and record the final version.

## 25. Quality review and measurement

### 25.1 Creative review

- [ ] The work feels light, warm, contemporary and restorative.
- [ ] Olive creates a clear anchor without overwhelming the ivory canvas.
- [ ] Logo artwork is official and legible.
- [ ] Flow uses an approved shape and does not compete with content.
- [ ] Photography shows authentic care or a meaningful customer moment.
- [ ] Skin, hair, hands, reflections and equipment look believable.
- [ ] The four pillars have distinct visual meanings where used.
- [ ] Copy explains a useful benefit or decision clearly.
- [ ] Service details, price and claims are verified.
- [ ] The next step is visible and accurately described.
- [ ] Contrast, captions, keyboard access and responsive crops are checked.
- [ ] Assets have appropriate rights and editable masters are retained.

### 25.2 Recognition test

Show campaign compositions without the logo to a small relevant audience. Ask what they remember, which brand they associate them with and what service they believe is offered. Record confusion with other spas. Test the palette, Flow and photography together before assuming that any one asset is distinctive or legally ownable.

### 25.3 Performance evaluation

| Objective | Useful measures | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Qualified reach, initial video retention and watch time | Whether the opening earns attention |
| Consideration | Saves, useful comments, treatment-page visits and engaged visits | Whether people seek more information |
| Booking | Form completion and confirmed appointments | Whether interest becomes an appointment |
| Return | Repeat booking within a defined service-appropriate interval | Whether the actual experience is worth repeating |
| Recognition | Unaided/assisted recall and source-of-discovery feedback | Whether people connect the work with KOMO |

Keep contact clicks, form submissions and confirmed bookings separate. Define denominators and periods before comparison. Review creative alongside audience, placement, offer and service changes. Avoid attributing repeat visits to art direction alone.

## 26. Reusable creative briefs

### 26.1 Photography brief

> Create a KOMO Botanical Sanctuary photographic story grounded in the actual outlet. Use warm ivory architecture, pale timber, green accents, broad curves and gentle light. Show attentive consultation and authentic scalp care with believable posture and equipment. Preserve natural skin and hair texture. Capture wide, medium and close scenes with space for responsive crops. Communicate being understood, comfortable, confident and refreshed. Use the real logo, products and facilities. Deliver originals, approved selects, usage information and an organised shot list.

### 26.2 Website implementation brief

> Apply this guide to the existing KOMO website. Begin by mapping current components and content to the proposed tokens. Preserve approved information and working functions. Use ivory as the main canvas, olive for contrast and actions, a warm sans serif for information and a restrained serif for short editorial headings. Build clear treatment choices and an accessible appointment journey. Derive Flow from the outlet architecture once master artwork is approved. Validate mobile crops, contrast, keyboard interaction, reduced motion, service facts and booking confirmation behaviour. Report unresolved assets or facts rather than inventing them.

### 26.3 Social campaign brief

> Develop a cohesive KOMO campaign around one customer motivation and one main action. Choose a pillar: Health, Joy, Beauty or Vitality. Use the approved template, upper-right logo and current palette. Show the actual service or an authentic human moment. Keep one main idea per asset. Pair sensory appeal with useful explanation across the campaign. Review claims and terms, preview platform crops, and retain editable masters. Evaluate attention, consideration and confirmed appointments separately.
